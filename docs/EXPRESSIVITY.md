# Teacher-referenced expressivity of the Kronecker additive output family

This analysis asks how far the fixed feature space of the additive head is from the next-token distributions of
a strong model. It uses saved checkpoints and per-position losses only; no model was trained for it. The four
4,096-position fit matrices ran the CPU fitting code on Modal L4 GPUs (about $2 of credit); everything else ran
on a CPU. Tables between `<!-- table:… -->` markers are generated by `scripts/build_summary.py` from
`results/expressivity/`.

Evidence labels used below: *recomputed* means re-executed from saved arrays by
`scripts/expressivity/audit_evidence.py` or `check_fits.py`; *fitted* means the achieved objective of a numerical
fit; *inferred* marks interpretation.

## 1. Question

KAS-P, the additive head with a fixed log-unigram prior and no trainable per-token term, is 0.2321 BPB behind the
prior-matched dense head. The trained models cannot say whether a better body, more width or different training
would close that gap, or whether the head's fixed feature space is itself far from what a strong model predicts.
This analysis measures the second possibility against concrete reference ("teacher") distributions. It does not
decompose language-model loss into causes.

## 2. Method: projection onto a fixed feature family

For a finite vocabulary, a fixed bias `b` and a fixed feature matrix `Φ` with rows `φ_v`, define

```
q_s(v) = exp(φ_vᵀ s + b_v) / Σ_u exp(φ_uᵀ s + b_u),        F_{Φ,b}(p) = inf_s KL(p ‖ q_s).
```

Every model whose logits lie in this affine feature space, whatever produces `s(x)` from the context, satisfies

```
E_x CE(p_x, q_{s(x)})  ≥  E_x H(p_x) + E_x F_{Φ,b}(p_x).
```

The bound is relative to `p`. The objective is convex in `s` (the gradient is `Φᵀ(q − p)`, the Hessian the
feature covariance under `q`). This is fixed-embedding softmax projection, treated explicitly by
[Ganea, Gelly, Bécigneul & Severyn (ICML 2019)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/ganea19a.html), section 3(b),
Theorem 2: fixed word embeddings, a free context vector, minimum cross-entropy and its maximum-entropy dual.
Adding a fixed base measure and inserting deterministic codec rows does not make a new theorem. What is specific to
this repository is the application: the exact codec mapping, the measurements against trained teachers, the
matched-position comparison with the trained heads, and the screen of alternative fixed codes.

## 3. The exact KAS-P feature family

`src/kq5/heads.py` gives KAS-P the logit

```
z_v = A_{L_v} Σ_{j ∈ S_v} (W_out h)_j  +  B_{L_v} Σ_j (W_out h)_j  +  ℓ_{L_v}  +  β_v
```

where `S_v` are the token's occupied byte-position cells, `A_L` and `B_L` are the codec's length-dependent
constants (from the occupancy construction, standard deviation and epsilon), `ℓ` holds 33 shared length biases and
`β` is a fixed buffer of centred add-one log-unigram counts. The matching feature family has 1,753 occupied cells
weighted by `A_v / A_ref`, one `B_v / B_ref` column and 33 length indicators: 1,787 features of numerical rank
1,333 (the rank of the code matrix itself, `results/setup/code_rank_gpt2bpe.json`).

*Recomputed:* codec logits and feature logits agree over all 57,243 tokens to 1.63e-5 (forward) and 5.00e-7
(reverse); the differences come from float32 feature weights, and the algebraic mapping is exact.

**A relaxation, not attainability.** The fit gives every context its own coefficients, including length
coefficients. A trained KAS-P shares `W_out`, has width 512, shares length biases and must compute coefficients
from the context. Dropping those restrictions enlarges the feasible set, which keeps the direction of the bound but
does not show that the trained architecture can produce all fitted distributions jointly. KAS-U and KAS-G heads
have additional per-token freedom and are not bounded by this family. Teacher and fitted distributions normalise
over the full training vocabulary, including EOS.

## 4. Teachers and positions

* **Teachers.** The two Dense-prior models with seeds 1 and 2 (`p4-densep-s1`, `p4-densep-s2`; 59.3M parameters;
  the four-seed mean is 1.3987 test BPB).
* **Positions.** In each split, 32 evenly spaced evaluation windows × 128 evenly spaced positions, the same grid
  for both teachers. This is a deterministic grid, not 4,096 independent draws, and the trained comparator is
  always KAS-P seed 1 (`m3-kasp-s1`).
* **Array audit (recomputed).** All four fit archives reproduce their stored means; target ids match the dev and
  test streams; the historical arms' NLL vectors match the saved per-position arrays exactly at those positions.
* **EOS targets.** The evaluator credits EOS with zero bytes, so BPB averages exclude it. The fitting script
  tested raw spelling length instead, and its stored summaries
  (`results/expressivity/gpu/floor_gpu_*.json`) include 5 dev and 4 test EOS targets. The tables below
  re-aggregate over non-EOS targets only (4,091 dev, 4,092 test); only the outer average changes, not the softmax
  support. Use `results/expressivity/evidence_audit.json` for the corrected values.

Nats per non-EOS target; the fitted family's bias is fixed to KAS-P's log-unigram prior, so this is exactly the

**Reading (inferred).** Under the stated fitting protocol, the best fit found in the KAS-P family stays about 0.74
nats from either teacher on either split, and the two teachers agree to within about 0.001 nats. On the same
positions, the teacher-fitted distribution has target NLL 0.098–0.106 nats below the trained KAS-P on dev and
0.010–0.011 below it on test. Descriptive window-cluster standard errors for that last difference are about
0.024–0.026 (dev) and 0.036 (test); the grid is deterministic, so these are not population confidence intervals.
The measurements are consistent with a substantial structural limitation of the KAS family relative to these
reference models. They do not show that the trained KAS-P is at an optimum, and they do not show equality on test.

The same fit with the teacher's learned bias in place of the log-unigram prior gives 0.740–0.743 nats. "About
0.74" is the right summary; a three-decimal range must say which bias it uses.

## 6. Four quantities that must not be conflated

| Quantity | Meaning | What it cannot establish |
|---|---|---|
| `F(p_teacher)` | optimal forward KL to this teacher within a specified affine family | optimal loss against the unknown language distribution |
| `−log q_fit(y)` | target loss of an oracle fitted to teacher probabilities | best achievable target loss in the family |
| `−log q_KASP(y)` | target loss of one trained model | its distance from the teacher-KL optimum |
| `inf CE(r_true, q)` | the population optimum | not observed here |

For the actual conditional distribution `r`, the target-loss gap of a projection `q*` is

```
CE(r, q*) − CE(r, p) = F(p) + Σ_v (r_v − p_v) log(p_v / q*_v).
```

The last term has unknown sign and size, and a one-target loss adds sampling noise, so matching a teacher KL to a
real-data gap does not split that gap into "representation" and "learning". No percentage of KAS-P's deficit is
attributed to representation here. The same logic applies to the stored-correction heads: their realised gaps are
below their row-set imitation values (§9), which shows that real-target loss and teacher imitation are different
objectives. A larger body cannot beat the teacher-family bound against the teacher, but it may still improve
language-model loss within the family.

These are achieved objectives of fits, not trained models.

* Every enriched row is given **the teacher's learned bias** and is fitted to the teacher's full distribution. A
  model trained on language targets with a fixed count prior has neither, so the table is not a ranking of
  trainable heads.
* The free-bias row refits a fresh bias for each 512-context chunk: a relaxation with eight independent
  intercept vectors, not one shared bias.
* More features means more capacity, which is the plain explanation for lower KL. The equal-size hashed identity
  family fits better than hashed trigrams, so the results do not identify local byte conjunctions as the cause of
  the gap; they measure what each feature set can express, not whether a body can learn to use it.
* Orderings depend on the target. On the model-free bigram target the 4,000-private-cell family (0.189) is better
  than trigrams (0.216); against the contextual teachers the order is reversed.

## 10. A same-length log-odds identity

Within one length class the codec's constants are shared, so `φ_a − φ_b = A_L (1[S_a] − 1[S_b])`. If
`φ_a − φ_b = φ_c − φ_d`, then for every additive head and every context

```
log(q_x(a)/q_x(b)) − log(q_x(c)/q_x(d)) = b_a − b_b − b_c + b_d.
```

*Recomputed census* over the 8,000 most frequent tokens: 19,950 same-length single-substitution pairs in 8,715
(length, position, byte → byte) groups; 3,263 groups hold two or more pairs (14,498 pairs). Examples:
` of`/` on` with ` if`/` in`; ` she`/` the` with ` son`/` ton`. Under empirical bigram conditionals the identity
is violated with a median spread of 2.16 nats across qualifying contexts (105 groups; descriptive, not a
significance test). The identity is a corollary of linear dependence and illustrates the restriction; it does
not account for the measured KL.

The identity also shows that heads with independent byte slots are not the same family as KAS across lengths:
their logits use unit occupancy weights, while the codec scales by `A_L`, and length intercepts cannot undo a
multiplicative length scaling.

For a minimisation, a feasible numerical objective is an upper estimate of the unknown infimum, not a certified
lower bound; no primal–dual certificate is stored. The 512-context refits and the model-free checks (unigram
LBFGS 1.3482 against Adam 1.3475; bigram 0.5525 to 0.5504 at doubled iterations) support the scale of the base
KAS effect. The independent float64 check on four positions, chosen before their outcomes were seen, found a much
larger residual improvement for the trigram family than for KAS, so the enriched-family objectives are not
converged to the third decimal. The KAS result is robust to this; its exact third decimal is not certified.

The 8,192 trigram buckets are features (6,299 of them occupied by 257,003 trigram occurrences in the
vocabulary), not 8,192 parameters. A width-512 projection for them allocates 4,194,304 more shared weights, so
at this vocabulary that head would have more trainable output parameters and Adam state than KAS-U64. Its
advantage is only that it does not grow with V. Counts are arithmetic for a stated implementation and exclude
buffers, activations and allocator overhead. Direct scoring still needs O(V) normalisation. No trigram head was
trained; its learnability and trained quality are untested.

## 13. What this analysis does not establish

* A new projection theorem, a new fixed-feature softmax architecture, or a general diagnostic principle.
* A causal decomposition of language-model error, or any percentage of KAS-P's deficit due to representation.
* That optimisation cannot help, or that KAS-P is at its true language-model floor: the measurement is relative
  to two teachers of one architecture and to a relaxed family.
* A certified constant: 0.74 is a numerical estimate on a fixed sample.
* Any trained-performance ranking of the enriched families.
* That cheap exact normalisation cannot encode frequency (§8), that independent-slot heads are the KAS family
  (§10), that sharper targets always have a larger floor, or that feature-family orderings are the same for every
  target (§7).
